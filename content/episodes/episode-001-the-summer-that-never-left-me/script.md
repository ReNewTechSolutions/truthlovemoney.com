## Spoken Script

Sometimes a poem does not simply give us words.

Sometimes it quietly opens a door.

Recently, I reread a poem I have loved for many years, and almost before I reached the end of the first lines, I felt myself carried back to summers I thought I had nearly forgotten.

Not forgotten completely, of course. The important memories do not really leave us. They wait. They settle somewhere deep inside the heart, patient and quiet, until something tender wakes them again.

For me, this time, it was a poem.

It brought back the warmth of childhood summers. Long bright afternoons. The sound of water. The laughter of family and friends. The feeling that a day could stretch on forever, as if summer itself had no ending.

I remembered swimming pools, and the way water holds so many kinds of memory. The splash of children playing. The shimmer of sunlight. The smell of towels warming in the sun. Voices calling from across the yard. The simple happiness of being together.

Later in life, I watched my parents enjoy the swimming pool they had worked so hard to build. At the time, those moments may have seemed ordinary. A sunny afternoon. A chair beside the water. A conversation. A little laughter.

But now I understand that ordinary moments are often the ones that become priceless.

That is one of the gifts of memory. It takes what we once passed through quickly and hands it back to us with meaning.

The poem also reminded me of teaching literature.

One of the things I loved most about teaching was watching students discover that literature is not simply something we read. It is something we experience.

Every reader brings a different life to every page.

A poem can sit in a classroom, printed in the same words for everyone, and yet it can open a different door in every person who reads it. One student may hear longing. Another may hear courage. Another may remember a grandmother, a summer, a first loss, a first hope.

That is the mystery and beauty of literature.

It does not belong only to the writer.

Once it reaches the reader, it becomes a conversation.

And sometimes, if we are lucky, it becomes a mirror.

Certain poems never leave us because they meet us in more than one season of life. We read them when we are young, and they mean one thing. We return to them years later, and suddenly they have changed.

But maybe the poem has not changed at all.

Maybe we have.

We bring more life to it. More love. More loss. More gratitude. More questions. And the poem, somehow, is still there waiting for us.

That is why I believe every story has something to teach us.

Some lessons come through joy. Some come through heartbreak. Some come through books, poems, music, family stories, photographs, and ordinary days that become meaningful only after time has passed.

A favorite kitchen utensil can hold a whole childhood.

A ring from a parent can carry a lifetime of love.

A worn book can remember every hand that held it.

These things become more than objects.

They become chapters.

And our memories, when we pay attention to them, become teachers.

The summer that never left me is not only about a swimming pool, or childhood, or a poem.

It is about the way life keeps speaking to us through the things we thought were small.

It is about the truth that our stories matter.

It is about the love that remains.

It is about the wisdom that keeps blooming long after the moment has passed.

So today, I want to invite you to think about one memory that still lives in your heart.

Maybe it is a summer afternoon.

Maybe it is a book.

Maybe it is someone you loved.

Maybe it is a song, a place, a sentence, or a small object tucked away in a drawer.

Ask yourself: what does this memory still have to teach me?

Because every life holds chapters worth remembering.

Every experience can become a seed of wisdom.

And every story, if shared with love, has the power to encourage someone else.

Welcome to The Lyon Den.

Never stop learning.
